### What is this and why am I doing this?

Hello. This is a monthly newsletter update thingi that I'd like to do to stay more connected with my friends. It is also my attempt at vulnerability, which I'm working on. If you are reading this, you are important to me and our friendship is something I value. Thank you for taking the time out.

This will also not be good writing, but it will be simple and unnuanced.

**Also - feel free to reply/not reply at your own pace/convenience.** The goal with this is not to force conversation or engage. It is to asynchronously, honestly and (within reasonable bounds) meaningfully remain "in touch" or connected with people I care about.

### Pills, powders, papers

If April (especially the Sikkim trip and its focal point, the trek) was a celebration of an invincible and united mind, body and spirit, May was the undoing of my hubris. The seemingly endless drove of pills in their different shapes and sizes have proved ineffective against the determined parasite(s) that have occupied my gut. No doubt, this persistent infection is enabled by my lack of pill-popping discipline, stemming from a major distrust of doctors and big pharma etc etc. After weeks of dilly-dallying, however, I am (hopefully) at the edge of ableness: a final combination of antibiotics, probiotics, prebiotics and god-knows-what-tics should expunge whatever nonsense is inhabiting my gut.

All of May was spent either a layer of dust off every edifice in my house, or a layer of sweat off myself. While the dust seemed endemic, the sweat was Bombay-versal. The rising temperature correlated with a universal edginess and frustration in a month where little went right, the doldrums of a year that has felt like a week.

Between the pills and the powder, a growing pile of paper(work) and related logistics cut my antibiotic addled brain and dust-infected body one thousand times over, with its attested xerox copies, fuckall websites and beaureacracy.

### Running from, running to

A year on from attempting intention, lo-fi contours are beginning to noodle around a pencil-drawn life-design map. As I [wrote](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/record/may-june-25) last June (at the peak of post-vipassana-clarity):

![](/images/may-26/running-to.png)

Instead of running from glass ceilings and fabricated constraints (as I am used to), this experiment with intentionality has pushed me to run to, rather than from. The endpoint of this running is still hazy and obscure (as I expect it to be), but the aforementioned contours provide sufficient directional validation. This internal validation, to my surprise, is visceral and perceptible, making it a more sustainable compass.

It appears that part 1 (and year 1) of this intentionality experiment has been a success: finding myself in a position where these contours can take their own shape, and a playground to continue to do what _feels_ right rather than what seems right. If year 1 has been about reaching the starting line of a race worth running, I assume that year 2 (and beyond) is when I actually have to start running. While the jaggedness and stop-start-ness of the past 2 years in Bombay have been pivotal in getting to the starting line, it is only in May and June that I have gotten used to running again. My internship at Kulfi has (amongst a lot of very good things) warmed up parts of my brain (as I nursed parts of my body) for the infinite race that finally seems to be starting.

### Everything is romantic

Buoyed by the success of my intentionality experiment, I have decided on my next experiment: delusion.

![](/images/may-26/delusion-chat.png)

Intentionality helped filter out signal from noise, and discern speed from velocity-- helpful starting points. Yet, to actualise this theoretical world/life building, I suspect that delusion may be the only way to endure this general state of [reflexive impotence](https://socialecologies.wordpress.com/2017/01/16/mark-fisher-on-depressive-hedonia/) (being aware of the sad state of the world but paralysed by hopelessness). My personal definition of delusion is an unwavering belief in exceptionalism -- [that rules don't apply](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/record/march-april-26) and that [anything can be done](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/record/october-november-25). The ethos of this definition is old-school Silicon Valley techno-optimism, where pathological optimism is necessary for founder/startup types to endure, persevere and innovate in the face of incumbents.

The risk with this experiment is that the delusion extends only to thought, and not to action. So, as the Silicon Valley OGs did, and the modern day delusion experts (found on Instagram reels and Substack notes) do, I am figuring out tangible avenues for this delusion to manifest.

So far, it has worked in convincing myself that I actually want to quit smoking cigarettes. Next, I will see if I can delude myself into taking personal branding seriously and get over the unfathomable, insurmountable cringe of posting on LinkedIn.

### Asymptote bag

The past ~20 sutta-less days have improved my tolerance for tension. I have learnt that quitting cigarettes is an asymptotic process, that you never really quit cigarettes, you just learn how to love them from a non-zero distance.

![](/images/may-26/asymptote.png)

I guess in the spirit/habit of romanticising everything, there is some beauty in yearning for a Benson Light, especially in the drudges of the post-post-summer we are in. On the other hand, there is much beauty in asymptotes: the perpetual shrinking distance between two things, tension drenched and unprovable.

Sadly, as a result of not smoking cigarettes, my creative output has jumped off a cliff (nothing asymptotic about this). Writing this (or writing anything, for that matter) has been agonising and uncharacteristically difficult without my lil nic hit. Miss u :(
