### Rule immunity

In continuing the theme of the [last edition](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/record/december-25-january-february-26) (a decade that ended the same way it started), I went to Sikkim for a vacation, just as I did in April 2016. Going into the trip, the parallels between the two decade-apart trips were inescapable: largely the same group of co-conspirators, the same exact trek and even the same trekking bag. I even bought an mp3 player, found my old hard-disk and emulated playlists and taste from what I imagined I listened to when I was 17.

Now that mid 20s meaning-making is also inescapable, the lonely hours spent trekking were spent trying to squeeze sense out of why I was doing this to myself. Unlike 10 years ago where we blessed by clear skies and able bodies, the moody Himalayas greeted us with a precipitation-filled vengeance, determined to test our aging knees, tar-filled lungs and office-chair accustomed backs. Through the ascent, I was nagged with the unanswered question of why I signed up to 10 days of aches and pains and a hole for a bathroom in pursuit of climbing this mountain. 10 years ago, I could chalk this up to the folly of youth, but aged 27, why am I doing this??

On the day we summitted (idt this is an actual word), as the air began to thin and our water bottles began to freeze over, the answer, thankfully like the weather, became clear: ["because it is there"](https://www.notion.so/arnavmcr-gmail-com-22d46a679169807d9971e87bca8e591c?pvs=21). When asked by a NYT reporter ~100 years ago why he wanted to climb Mount Everest, Gerorge Mallory said "because it is there". In hindsight, this was the literal explanation that framed this trip and this trek. Generalised, I translate "because it is there" to "because it can be done", a meaningless pursuit of experience. As I wrote in the [last edition](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/record/december-25-january-february-26), being able to just do things because they can be done is something I value / crave.

Designing and subsequently building a life that enables being unencumbered and untethered seems fantastical, yet I am holding out hope that it can be done. Despite better judgement, I have drunk my own kool-aid ([an unrelenting belief in one's own exceptionalism](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/record/october-november-25) and being immune to rules), and the euphoria of doing this trek reinforced my hope that such life design is plausible.

Beyond the epiphanies and LinkedIn-style gyaan, Sikkim was a dense trip, packed with memories. Dull moments tended towards zero. Somehow even the most trivial acts prompted lengthy discussion and guarenteed laughter. These two weeks will live on with me forever, quite literally, since the four of us got tattoos to commemorate this trip, a celebration of friendship, exploration and freedom.

### Take me to your best friend's house

In the lead up to Sikkim, there was sobering feeling that these few weeks may be the last few where I could still breathe. The impending doom of the next ~15 months and the relentlessness of travel, study and life already planned out (to varying degrees) added a finiteness to March, that as soon it was done, life would begin again and would not stop for some time. This doom manifested itself, conversely, as a party.

![](/images/march-april-26/27_march_invite_square_(1).png)

The theme 'Bombay Retrospective' also represented this awkward period, and the party was an attempt at looking back before looking ahead. In this sense, that Friday night was congruent with the experience-rich time I've had since moving back to Bombay: romance-adjacent tanglements (and unentanglements), suspicious ziplocs, crowdsourced art and pale blue sunrises among the leading indicators.

As we (the Sheth household) has learnt through the last couple of years of hosting, inevitably the soul of parties becomes one specific room. This room is where I've lived (and referenced many many times) since I moved back. The atmosphere of nostalgia applies specifically to this room too, since it is being renovated as we speak. Of the many things I love about my house, this room is leaps and bounds above everything else.

Its two single-beds at other ends of the room scaffolds conversation, instigating but never forcing eye contact, agnostic of group size. It's almost always lit up by a singular IKEA tall diffuser, with just enough light to see the paraphernalia my sister and I stubbornly stuck to either walls. For a brief time, a short guessing game was inevitable every time someone new entered the room: basis the stuff on the wall, whose side is which?

![](/images/march-april-26/IMG_0850.jpeg)

![](/images/march-april-26/ed440d1b-8367-4fab-ab58-685d0d21c41a.png)

Yet the focal point of the room is the window: (previously) perenially equipped with an ashtray and a slew of mostly non-functional lighters. Unlike the dramatic sunsets and the Arabian Sea horizon that the West-facing windows offer, the bulk of the view from this window is directly into other buildings, mostly far enough to be able to only discern happenings, not individuals. This literal window into other worlds, the room's permanent warm glow and its general quietness ensured that time froze, every single day.

![middle of the night and trusted lamp in full form](/images/march-april-26/IMG_0795.jpeg)

The room suspended the rest of the world in a way that no other space has. For all of the lockdown, after a day of attempted homemaking and fights, my sister and I would stare out of the window and speak indefinitely, shoring over the tension of co-existing and the world at large. This quality carried through these years, coalescing into a 'safe space', I would like to think for anyone who set foot in it. Thank you to everyone who painted this room with your ideas, thoughts and feelings -- I already miss the music, the smoke and the laughter.

### Eye of the storm, belly of the beast, bite of the kulfi

My first commitment in the post-Sikkim world is a return to the formal workplace, payslips, WFO and all. I am working at [Kulfi Collective](http://kulficollective.com) for the next couple of months. Kulfi talks like a magazine, dresses like a tech company and walks like an advertising agency (which is what it is, albeit a premium, tech-forward and culture-forward one).

So far, it is a place where all my pattern recognition has failed. Startup-speak, or even consulting-speak is ineffective. The tendency to default to data as a source of truth also does not apply. (Some) people take an actual one hour lunch break.

In the midst of my brain adjusting to this markedly foreign world, the evolving backdrop of the marketing/advertising industry makes this a particularly crucial juncture for the company.

Within white collar work, there has been much fanfare around how IT services companies (a large Indian export success story) like TCS or Infosys will be affected by the rise of AI. This is already playing out, visible in Infosys' bleak Q4 results. This entire industry (a big chunk of Scaler's customers) was built on labour arbitrage: being able to hire 'cheap' labour relative to the global market yet service global clients. Because labour was so relatively cheap, it allowed for generous staffing (even if marginal productivity of such large teams was stupendously low) who would pander to clients' beck and call. Now, with AI etc, having such large teams to perform rote IT tasks seems inefficient at best, and a mass white-collar job apocalypse at worst.

Structurally, the advertising world is in the same position. Clients are accustomed to large teams (an account director, an account manager, a project manager, a strategy POC, a creative director, an art director, a copywriter, a production sub-team etc etc) who bend over backwards to appease them. The paltry salaries paid out to agency employees, especially 'creatives', results in sufficient surplus value, perpetuating this system of labour arbitrage.

Unlike IT services, where the impact of AI seems obvious, measurable and vanilla, 'creatives' are a different breed. Their hubris (rightly so) comes from their mind, their process, their creation. This makes AIs role and impact more tenuous but equally pressing (their hubris-derived resistance does not change structural realities), in my view.

I am working on an 'AI transformation' (in consulting-speak) -- essentially building AI systems/processes and their adoption. At what feels like a flashpoint in this industry, this project (and internship), I hope, will solve for two objectives:

- Learning how to get people diametrically and structurally anti-AI to use it (if it can be done here, I suspect it can be done pretty much anywhere else)
- Gaining fluency in marketing-speak, a function I have rather stupidly ignored until Putt One

At a more personal level, this is my version of 'selling out' and working at a 'cool' place. There are at least 4 other people wearing band t-shirts on any given day. On my first day, someone presented to a room of 10 on the @lifeofpujaa e-lafda. The office's meeting room names are antiSocial, Bonobo, Soul Fry etc etc. Most amazingly there is a meeting room named Willingdon. So far, results of 'selling out' are inconclusive, but being (metaphorically) so far from home makes each day enriching and confounding in equal measure.

I expect this steep learning curve to continue as I spend more time in an industry that is forced to reckon with itself with each passing day.

### We've got the map backwards

This month, I'm hosting this newsletter thingi on what I hope becomes a more permanent 'world' I'm trying to build. For now, this psuedo-portfolio replaces my previous psuedo-portfolio ([https://coalesceweb.wordpress.com/](https://coalesceweb.wordpress.com/)). Currently, this hosts all my writing (even the embarassing stuff from a decade ago), and also my offline music library (in case you are trying to stop using Spotify and want to invest in analogue audio and more *intentional* music listening, feel free to browse [this library](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/music/vault.html) -- and I can send you links to download the music you want). Eventually, I hope for this to be a 'world', with different architecture and characters etc.

I am glad to be free from Notion, a piece of software I despise. As I wrote [last year](https://www.notion.so/May-June-25-23146a67916980a981caf5215a674efd?pvs=21), I've waited for "vibecoding" my whole life. Now that it's here and there is basically no barrier to entry nor any way to escape the dopamine cycle of accepting Claude Code edits, my usage of AI has become far more intentional and measured than a few months ago, in a last ditch effort to protect the knowledge that goes into knowledge work. It's also reframed my view on how work happens now: more relational, more soft and more vibes-based.

In a world where work increasingly appears to be less skill-based and more trust-based, it has forced me to rethink where value comes from. I believe the highest source of value is now relationships. I built Tether to help with this: to be able to map out who you spend time with, and how you spend it.

I've been using it for a couple of months now, and it's helped me take stock of my time and life more. If you'd like to try it out, here's the link - [tetherme.lovable.app](http://tetherme.lovable.app) (landing page branding is a different name whooops will change once I figure out which name I like more).

With each Github push, the goal is to help navigate the increasingly relationship-based world of knowledge work and the many shapes it takes.

Some kinds of people it would help most: high flux environments (incoming Masters students hehe), networking-heavy roles (VCs) or blurred jobs (freelancers, founders).
