### What is this and why am I doing this?

Hello. This is a monthly newsletter update thingi that I'd like to do to stay more connected with my friends. It is also my attempt at vulnerability, which I'm working on. If you are reading this, you are important to me and our friendship is something I value. Thank you for taking the time out.

This will also not be good writing, but it will be simple and unnuanced.

**Also - feel free to reply/not reply at your own pace/convenience.** The goal with this is not to force conversation or engage. It is to asynchronously, honestly and (within reasonable bounds) meaningfully remain "in touch" or connected with people I care about.

---

### Cosplay

It is ironic that this exercise started to keep in touch with people who were away and in the brief period that I have been removed from normal life, it has come to a standstill. Still, I hold on hope that I will return to some feasible cadence as novelty fades and a new normal takes shape.

Much of these months were spent cosplaying: as an SF ~tech~ AI-bro, an MBA student, a post-MBA student, a wilful member of the diaspora, a prospective MBA student, an endowed travel writer and many many other things. The weeks of travel across June and July allowed me to see the world in its touristic glory, committed but untethered, engaged but bereft of consequence.

I previously viewed this dissonance between travel and tourism harshly. From 2020, after my year-ish long sojourn in South East Asia:

_In particular, my innate need to be a 'traveler' not a 'tourist' has recurred as a theme through these months. The constant need to prove that experiences are more than mere photo-ops and rackets, and to truly engage with the context that we're surrounded with is something I am determined to do, however cynical the agenda may seem. Filtering the noise that surrounds travelling, the glamour that surrounds working and the disdain that surrounds touring is both frustrating and exhausting, yet is something essential if we are to strip ourselves of appearances and lend ourselves to emotion._

-- Black t-shirts and bourgeoise guilt, 2020

Even in my annual use-my-accumulated-leaves-to-avoid-burnout month-long travels after the pandemic, the objective was always immersion: spending time to feel as localised as possible, days of daily rigmarole in foreign lands with the occasional museum or gig to justify travelling halfway across the world. This year was more touristic in a literal sense (checking things off a list), yet the list was far more meaningful and more enjoyable than ever before. No longer am I concerned about the authenticity of experience (it is becoming clearer that that is a losers game, a race to the bottom), but I am increasingly secure in my taste and still sufficiently open to the spontaneous combustion that 'authentic' travel requires. This selfish shift allowed me to do things I've wanted to, a recurring objective that refuses tradeoffs. From March 2025, after watching Green Day play Lollapalooza:

_if anything and within reasonable bounds, watching Green Day, or going to Stamford Bridge or doing any of the other seemingly dumb things I wanted to do before my pre-frontal cortex had developed, are now things I am going to try do more of._

So watching American Football play at the Brooklyn Paramount, visiting Kurt Cobain's memorial in Seattle or fulfilling my Tumblr-girlie dream of doing a road trip in the Pacific North West while listening to Bon Iver, the 1975, and Death Cab for Cutie are similar limbic ruptures to my brain, spectacular and etched deep in my brain.

### So here we are

If July was fraught with consecutive mini-ruptures (it was, and I wrote about it in an only half ironic article titled ["Around the World in 80 Raves"](https://oh-messy-life.vercel.app/writing/around-the-world-in-80-raves)), the past month in Singapore has been in anticipation of ruptures, eager for the MBA and the city to subsume me and overwhelm me.

My situationship with MBAs has been ongoing; an on-and-off will-they-won't-they for a while now, and in this period I've unintentionally reaped the benefit of going last. I've bided my time till most around me have, or are, doing their Masters degree (as we are taught to)-- now being able to learn from their collective wisdom. I've come equipped with algorithms on the constant social calculation (which I am trying to forget), the impossible holy trinity of grades, social life and career outcome (any guesses on which i intend to deprioritise?), egregiously high alcohol consumption and a now waning tolerance for top 40 music (who would win in a cage match between top 40 club music and Bollywood music?).

Still, I anticipate, waiting for this profound epiphany that will confirm I am doing my Masters degree, I am living away from home and I am essentially rebuilding life in a somewhat profuse way. I guess I'm attuned to measuring beginnings in days and times and moments, but most times they are less precise. Beginnings, like endings, are results. The result of everything that came before, microdecisions and macroeconomics, serendipity and effort.

So here we are: back in my spiritual homeland (South East Asia), where we drink beer like its water and smoke cigarettes like our GPA depends on it, where we gossip like we're on reality TV and watch reality TV as if real life is somehow less ridiculous, where we say good morning and smile before exams, grateful for the Z-curve (relative grading for the uninitiated), where we plan travels like we're Magellan and where we yearn for the future like it's watching, waiting and calling.

### Flirtation

So if I'm here, in the now, then what happens? Most days I continue my cosplaying, with a tonic of futurism: maybe I will try to live in Singapore and become a fintech bro, maybe I will try to live in London and make peace with working till I am 100 years old, or, in an even darker, scarier scenario, I will become a management consultant (for a change I am really not being facetious); or I return to India to do some third thing, and make peace with spending mornings at the Willingdon and nights at antiSocial. It is unclear and only half within control, still it is fun to flirt with, to dance with and to know it's all asymptotic.
