#### Wherever you go, there you are

If I spent September in anticipation of some big grand confirmation of a beginning, October (and November) was a stream of reminders that the beginning has already begun. In moments of sobriety, between my colour-coded calendar and endless to-do list, it is clear that this is it: I have been subsumed and swept, been bent almost to breaking. I am here, there I am.

I leave my laundry in the dryer for days. My weekly flower runs have become fortnightly, the lantanas wilt in a bottle of Bombay Sapphire next to our house memory board, now requiring manicuring for any contribution. My backpack's strap broke and it took me 3 weeks to fix it, I still have not set up my Singapore bank account and the pre-drinks beer from Diwali before I threw on my one kurta for the third time that week lies mostly full by my bedside still, draped in fairy lights, boarding passes and past exam papers.

My DJ controller is now our DJ controller, and the student council's speaker is now my speaker. Our grocery shopping has regressed from eggs, bread, chilli oil and Nespresso pods to Nespresso pods and a 10-pack of Tiger. Leisurely cigarette breaks have been replaced by timestamped, precise speed smoking meetings. No longer are we bound by the shackles of smalltalk and teach-me-how-to-curse-in-your-language, we've progressed to the ethics of working at McKinsey and who should win the Ballon d'Or.

And in all this beginning and carpe dieming, it is now almost over. Ended, akhir, la fine, khalaas. In three-ish weeks I will trade the Indian Ocean for the Arabian Sea, and attempt to reassimilate into a world I've mostly forgotten and definitely neglected in these months, until (assuming the visa gods bless me) I trade my Bombay breakfasts (of black coffee and a cigarette) for French breakfasts (of black coffee and a cigarette). What choice exists but to refuse this finiteness for the next three weeks, and accelerate this bubble whose bursting is time-stamped and earmarked.

#### I <3 the underground

Even if my lifelong search for the hipster cool and the fabled authenticity of some unknown underground is a race to the bottom (as I wrote a few months ago), it is becoming clearer to me that it is an infinite game: the search itself is the hipster cool and the game itself is the fable.

If all of October was confirmation of being subsumed as I so craved, I found some recluse in the dark rooms and hardstyle techno of the _underground_ at Pawnshop in Taipei. A night filled with commitment, experience and being tethered (as I prescribed some months ago in Around the World in 80 Raves) confirmed that my life's purpose is perhaps not to be in management consulting. Instead, it is to peel the stickers placed on my phone camera and navigate out of a room of half-naked men to watch the sun rise beyond the empty streets in a foreign land.

#### Chat am I the diaspora?

On Thursday, we put up fairy lights, bought flowers and printed teen-patti rules.

On Saturday, I was designated shopper for some Italians breaking their kurta-virginity. I spent the day listening to Baalti and Anish Kumar, and reading Rahul's pieces on the desi electronic continuum ([here](https://aftereod.substack.com/p/disorienting-the-underground-part) and [here](https://aftereod.substack.com/p/disorienting-the-underground-part-efe))

Manan and I cosplayed being truly back home (aka the Willingdon), eating our dosas and drinking our fresh lime juice in Little India.

At night, I played Baalti and Anish Kumar for 30-mins before being inundated by requests to play Bollywood.

![](/images/october-november-26/03.jpg)

After many drunk calls, I listened to Prateek Kuhad and the F16s in my backyard.

On Sunday, I got a haircut and got dressed to go to a temple where I did not recognise any of the gods. After asking this unnamed figure for a high-paying job, we ate mutton sukka and prawn curry.

At night, we went for India's #1 Dancing DJ's Diwali party and danced to Bolly-tech. India's #1 Dancing DJ (also my school friend and former bandmate) showed much kindness and love when seeing us in the audience, inviting us 'backstage' to watch the diaspora do every hook step and scream every word, and see ourselves in it.

#### Principles and agents

It's unclear if we're living in a pre AI world or a post AI world, or if we're somewhere in the middle of this impending cognitive apocalypse. Much has been said already of AI's flattening power: it has a unique ability to take any input and regurgitate sameness, flattening any nuance.

Still, flattening is too kind: using AI feels like our brains are folding into themselves, with synapses collapsing and falling and flailing. A grand regression to the mean is upon knowledge work.

In some desperate attempt to out-manoeuvre the inevitable, I have taken to personal branding. From June 2026:

*So far, it (being delusional) has worked in convincing myself that I actually want to quit smoking cigarettes. Next, I will see if I can delude myself into taking personal branding seriously and get over the unfathomable, insurmountable cringe of posting on LinkedIn.*

The results of this experiment are inconclusive, but directionally positive. So like share subscribe or whatever the fuck

#### Family trees and olive branches

Even if the bubble never bursts, it is on occasion translucent enough to see outside. When I do, I see marriages and engagements, birthdays and badminton. Some days, the Tiger 10 packs and Tech Club events and football and assignments are rendered ineffective in forgetting the 'outside world'.

I may not miss it, and I may not remember it either, but if you see me bawling my eyes out next to exit A of the Holland Village MRT, it is on the days I look outside.
