This is meant to be a half-ironic, half-didactic reminder of a youth spent chasing the night, and being chased by it, written before I am swept up by the vanity of a Masters degree (that too an MBA). While the necessary self-importance of building a 'successful' career is not lost on me, as much as I disavow it and wish I could hipster-nonchalant my way out of my very obvious need for achievement, I reconcile it with a singular pursuit that has guided my youth: to chase the night. Far from the hedonistic connotation of the phrase, this is a more Bourdain-esque immersion, self-discovery and identity formation outsourced to immersion.

I write this as a framework with three components, to appease the MBA gods. Maybe I will impress them and they will grant me three wishes. If they did, I would choose to relive three Nights from July -- in New York, in London and in Bali.

## Commitment

While much of the post 9/11 post-punk scene in New York (the Strokes, Interpol, Yeah Yeah Yeahs etc) have long been markers of alternative music, LCD Soundsystem always eluded me. Despite repeated efforts to try and make sense of dancepunk (a portmanteau of two of my favourite things, dance and punk), the band seemed too pastiche-y, a whole far worse than the sum of its parts disguised in showmanship and hipster acclaim.

Yet somehow in the neighbourhood where James Murphy (founder of LCD Soundsystem) grew up, his slam poetry style delivery, the maracas and the arpeggios all came together. If LCD Soundsystem are to be believed (I have been converted after one transcendental night in Brooklyn), chasing the Night is a dogged pursuit, recursive and backward.

> *"And to tell the truth*
> *Oh, this could be the last time*
> *So here we go*
> *Like a sales force into the night"*
>
> *All My Friends, Sound of Silver (2007), LCD Soundsystem*

The first component of a Night is a commitment to it. A pledge to going out, to being with your friends, to have fun. No matter if your commitment comes from being submerged under a deluge of responsibility, or it comes from the anxiety of the finiteness or ephemerality of happenings or the touristic objective-orientedness of 'vasoolifying' or getting your money's worth (and then some); commitment is the necessary function for a Night. Without it, the Night does not exist. With it, the Night is yours to take.

Back in Bushwick, we wait patiently in line to enter Nowadays for Kilopatrah Jones' residency, expecting a night of sweat, diva house and ghetto tech. The half-hours pass but the queue is unmoved. Well past midnight, early cracks begin to reveal themselves: do we abandon Nowadays and go elsewhere (pun intended, the club is named Elsewhere)? We self-imposed a deadline of 1:15a, surely by then we would be inside Nowadays, and if not, we would make a belated exit to Elsewhere. 1:15a approaches and high spirits are now dampened by parched throats and an oral fixation quenched only by inhaling and blowing smoke. The queue moved only when a group cut their losses, getting into an Uber and accepting that today was not the day they would be entering Nowadays.

Somewhere around 1:45a, dehydrated and mildly deflated, we gave up our position in line, albeit unwillingly, skeptical of the expensive 'at the door' tickets and possible queue elsewhere. The two hours of queuing started with niceties, shifted into rave tale-telling among the four of us, and ended with an unspoken mutual recognition of our objective that Night: to commit to it, and let it happen to us.

Commitment to the night is to let it subsume you; to recognise that you are much smaller than it. This submission is freeing. Without hubris or ego or arithmetic to guide decisions, you absolve personal responsibility to the object itself, the Night, a proto-religious submission to the mystery and sacredness of the dark. In Mamdanistan, it is hard not to believe in proto-religion, a magical land where no rules apply, a city that smells of piss, weed and love, far removed from the bleakness and the anxiety us plebeian non-New Yorkers occupy ourselves with. This summer in New York, as evidenced by the Knicks, everything came true. All you had to do was believe it would come true, and let it happen.

![Elsewhere's dancefloor, Bushwick](/images/around-the-world-in-80-raves/elsewhere-dancefloor.jpg)

Finally inside Elsewhere, after window-shopping at other nearby venues, we danced because that is what we had set out to do, determined to move until their 4am closing time, after hours of standing still. The micro-annoyance of a Night, that in that moment, seemed to have been cut short led to a fervour that we carried onto the road outside Elsewhere. Sat on the street, we giggled and plotted our next move as we were joined by the perfect foil: an ethnically ambiguous man (later revealed that his parents were from Charni Road and Bandra) enamoured and curious about our lives and adventures with a shared penchant for celebrating 420 (America's greatest cultural export). He vanished into the night as we made our way to Bushwick Diner, what appeared to be the last stop on a Night that had already extended beyond anticipation. With the pale blue sky now above us, we were prepared to end the Night with hearts and stomachs satiated.

Commitment also entails trust, an implicit mutual understanding between participants of the Night to chase it as a collective, even if united by nothing more than this commitment. On a highly committed Night, when forks inevitably reveal themselves on a long, long road, consensus is hard to achieve. Still, faith in the collective, especially in times of disagreement, is a true demonstration of commitment. To further this collectivism, it is less about saying yes, and more about not saying no. If chasing the night is dogged and recursive, it requires refusal until it is conquered, staying united and together until there is nothing left.

> *"Though when we're running out of the drugs*
> *And the conversation's grinding away*
> *I wouldn't trade one stupid decision*
> *For another five years of life"*
>
> *All My Friends, Sound of Silver (2007), LCD Soundsystem*

With the outside world settling into their morning routines, in another act of proto-religion and refusal, we followed vague instructions from the Brooklyn-native Vespa-riding messianic champion among us, to trek across the borough to watch the sun rise above the Manhattan skyline. Even as our tobacco packets and patience wore thin, a ninety-minute game of pickup football was played with professional levels of commitment, burning through tiredness with a single-minded mission to never let this Night of spontaneous combustion end.

The subsequent weekend was borne out of this commitment, criss-crossing boroughs and never saying no, ending on the Third Day's sunset, the crowning and the comedown where we willingly accepted the Night and its end. We threw around our frisbee and we sat on the grass, we ate chips and we listened to Prateek Kuhad, at peace with our bodies, ourselves and the Night.

## Experience

The second component of a Night is experience: knowhow of how to approach, enjoy and come down from it; to not let it overwhelm you. This requires both explicit and tacit knowledge. Explicit knowledge facilitates a Night's logistics, wherein timings, transportation, tickets are in place to help frame the Night, without adding friction to its proceedings. While this factor of experience-building appears straightforward, it is fraught with points of failure, landmines that if ignored can risk the Night itself. Tacit knowledge is acquired through iteration and effort, an intuition sharpened through loops of exploration and reflection, distilled into 'taste'.

In London, we went for a day party ('darty' is the appropriate term, I have been informed) ironically named 'Night Service', where Ryan Elliott (Berghain resident, scene OG and most notably for my MBA dissonance, a former financial analyst) played a 4-hour sunset set (from 6 until 10pm) at Fold, a strictly no-phones-on-the-dancefloor venue in Canning Town.

Both explicit and tacit knowledge coalesced into a professional Night, planned to the T and executed with panache. At first, choosing this event (from the seemingly endless list of gigs happening in London that day) was a function of our weekend, not just that night. Well aware of a late Friday night and an early Sunday morning, an all-nighter seemed excessive. Previous triple-header weekends have left us (across continents) debilitated, so experience led us to a 'darty' as a much needed middle ground. Choosing 'Night Service' as the darty de jour was also straightforward: Ryan Elliott's tracks have found their way into my playlists over the years via Shazam, and "bro you have to listen to this track"-type introductions. I have also slowly drifted away from the in-your-face-ness of the bass scene, with house and techno soundtracking most rumination that your mid-late 20s forces upon you.

The day itself began with movement and nourishment. Our well-fought games of badminton and subsequent brunch ensured that our bodies were equipped with the endorphins and calories we needed. Working backward from our goal to reach Fold at 4:30pm, WhatsApp groups were activated, setting a 2pm kick-off for pre-drinks, well aware of the acceptable 30-minute window of delay. Outfits were selected, naps were taken, and cocktails were prepared with six-sigma levels of precision. The availability of substances was confirmed weeks in advance, kept apportioned and concealed as the conversation and tequila flowed. Google Maps was checked in 30-minute intervals, Uber in 15-minute intervals and tobacco pouches in 5-minute intervals.

At Fold, we did a now-standard lay-of-the-land, assessing smoking areas, bars and locating ourselves on the dancefloor. While substance preferences and timing had slight variance, our tenured gang felt no obligation to act out someone else's version of the night, each of us confident in our individual tacit knowledge cultivated through years of putting in the hard yards, whether as fake-ID and cheap vodka college kids, big-festival-chasing and able-bodied young professionals or jaded, 'intentional' late 20-somethings. This was our collective 10,000 hours, an adolescence spent committing to the Night with the scars, lungs and pupils to show for it.

Individual mastery (the pinnacle of Experience) is laudable and an impressive personal feat. Yet when it is enmeshed with other masters, the result is clockwork, even if not as magnificent. The diligence of adhering to timings, respecting others' decisions and appreciating the before and the after of a Night is only possible with earned confidence and security.

As the vocals *"I'm in love with the DJ, do you know....play my song Mr. DJ..."* (from ATB's 2003 classic "In Love with the DJ") faded in at 9:15pm, the metronomic dancing inched closer towards the Night's release, trance and house-induced celebration. As the Eurotrance built up into its final chorus, the last rays of the sunset flittered through the slits in Fold's walls, and both sunglasses and pretense were discarded in favour of dilated pupils and endless hugs. The triumph appeared fleeting, but was 10 years and countless Nights in the making.

## Tether

Nightlife and contemporary rave culture fixate on presentness: remaining fixated in the present moment, fleeting transcendence from past and future. Yet, the power of presentness is not in forgetting the past or escaping the future, it is in allowing both to bookend the Night: hedonism tethered to context. Nights are hardly accidental or cosmic, as hedonism would have you believe, they are borne out of intention, some latent desire to commit to the Night that only reveals itself on the dancefloor, or in the pit. Remaining rooted in this desire, no matter if it is borne out of frustration or jubilation or some third thing, enables the Night's healing quality, perhaps its most potent capability.

> *"The body and the land hold information. They hold the past and the future as they exist in the present. The land and the body know they have been colonized, they also remember that this was not the case and they know that one day they will no longer be colonized."*
>
> *Gavilán Rayna Russom*

To tether is to float, dancing between the scars of the past and the imagination of the future with both feet still in the present.

Half the world away from London, I found myself alone in Bali, after 30 days of travel that left me with a once-again uncooperative gut, a frightening credit card bill and emotions teeming across my body, surfaced but not free, where the tiniest slit would carve myself fully open. To come down from this month of adventure, I found Klymax Discotheque, a Friday-night-only basement club, touted by Resident Advisor to have one of the world's best sound systems. Being one of the few (or only) venues in Bali that played more than top 40 remixes from 2016 or 'afro'-'house' or choreographed disco edits, Klymax's programming presented much confidence in being the site of emotional processing through movement. I had committed to the Night months in advance, and structured my day around reaching the club by midnight, healthy, able and ready.

*"Aawaz it he behi hule..." (This sound is so beautiful...)* echoed the vocal loop on one of the world's best soundsystems, to a sparse, fog-filled room. "Calling", originally sung by British-Indian 'Sangeeta' in the 90s and later remixed by Turbotito and Ragz was the night's crescendo. An allegedly 'bhangra trance' track made by South Asian diaspora in the UK, being played by a Singaporean DJ collective in Indonesia to a mostly caucasian audience proved a fitting ending to a month of globalism before a year-ish of globalism.

Staying tethered to this imminent globalism and the emotional turbulence and oversocialisation of the month prior made for a Night that acted as a bridge between these two distinct periods. Tetheredness, combined with Commitment, results in 'sideways time': a temporal mode that is free from duration and expectation (Wark, 2023). In this state, there is no weight of history, nor is there any yearning for futures, all simply coexist, with memory and desire bleeding into each other.

The track's ("Calling") refrain, *"Aaoo naa, aaoo naa" (come, come)* was suspended in time, drenched in reverb that echoed and blinked off the singular giant disco ball at the head of a dancefloor which reoriented itself away from the DJ booth and toward each other, revolving in space where eyes locked and grins widened. With every *"Aaoo naa"*, I danced both to the beat and around the precipice of possibility that I had only tiptoed around until now, grateful for the past, within the embrace of the present and at peace with the future.

---

So what now of a decade-long pursuit of the Night? Perhaps the law of diminishing marginal utility will rear its ugly head, or I will learn to accept the encumbrance of compounding responsibilities, chipping away at the ability to commit, or perhaps being geographically removed from co-conspirators will take its toll. Until then, consider this an ode to a youth well spent.
